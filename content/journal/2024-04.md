# April — Rain & Renewal

It has rained every day this week. Not the angry, dramatic rain of summer storms, but the gentle, persistent kind that makes the world smell of earth and possibility.

I've been reading Haruki Murakami again. His books are like rain, in a way — they arrive quietly, soak into you, and leave everything subtly different.

## The Art of Slowing Down

Modern life has a terrible momentum. It pulls you forward constantly — the next task, the next notification, the next thing to consume. This month I've been practicing the art of slowing down. Not productivity hacks or time management systems. Just... pausing.

Five things I paused for this month:
1. Watching a spider build its web outside my window (forty-five minutes)
2. Listening to Satie's Gymnopédies three times in a row
3. Sitting in a park without my phone
4. Cooking a meal without rushing — chopping each vegetable with attention
5. Re-reading a poem until I could feel it, not just understand it

## On Friendship

Had a long conversation with an old friend this week. The kind of conversation where two hours pass and it feels like fifteen minutes. We talked about regret, ambition, and whether it's possible to be content without being complacent.

No conclusions. The best conversations don't have conclusions. They have openings.

> "The only way to do great work is to love what you do." — but also, sometimes, to simply show up when you don't.
