# May — The Garden Grows

Everything is green. Aggressively, unapologetically green. The garden has erupted into a chaos of growth that makes my careful winter planning look like the naive blueprints of someone who has never met a plant.

The tomatoes are already taller than I expected. The herbs are staging a quiet revolution in their raised bed. And something I didn't plant — I think it might be a wildflower — has appeared in the corner, bold and uninvited and beautiful.

## Lessons from Gardening

Gardening teaches you things that nothing else can:
- **Patience is not passive.** It's an active, attentive waiting.
- **Control is an illusion.** You can prepare the soil, but you can't make the rain fall.
- **Beauty often arrives unplanned.** The wildflower in the corner is more interesting than anything I deliberately planted.

## A Creative Breakthrough

The project I started in March has taken an unexpected turn. I was struggling with the structure — trying to force it into a shape it didn't want to be — and then I let go. Stopped trying to control it. And it bloomed, just like the garden.

Funny how the same lesson keeps arriving in different forms.

## Reading List This Month

- *Pilgrim at Tinker Creek* by Annie Dillard (a masterpiece of attention)
- *The Poetics of Space* by Gaston Bachelard
- *Braiding Sweetgrass* by Robin Wall Kimmerer (reading slowly, savoring)

Each of these books is, in its own way, about the same thing: the radical act of truly seeing what's in front of you.
