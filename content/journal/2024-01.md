# January Reflections

The new year arrives like a blank page in an old leather-bound journal. There's something profoundly hopeful about that — the untouched cream-colored paper, waiting.

I spent the first week of January in a kind of quiet observation. Watching snowflakes trace their spiraling descent against the grey sky outside my window. Each one a tiny, intricate universe dissolving on contact with the warm glass.

## Resolutions vs. Intentions

This year I've decided against resolutions. They feel too rigid, too binary — you either succeed or fail. Instead, I'm setting *intentions*. Soft guideposts rather than hard deadlines.

My intentions for this year:
- Read more fiction. Let stories rewire my empathy circuits.
- Write something every day, even if it's just a sentence.
- Spend more time in silence. Not the uncomfortable kind — the nourishing kind.

## A Quote That Stayed With Me

> "We do not remember days, we remember moments." — Cesare Pavese

I want to be more present for those moments this year. The way the morning light hits the bookshelf. The sound of rain at 3 AM. The warmth of a cup held between cold hands.
