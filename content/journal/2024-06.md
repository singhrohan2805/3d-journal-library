# June — Midsummer Light

The longest day of the year. I stayed outside until the last trace of light dissolved into the horizon, watching the sky cycle through its entire palette: gold, amber, rose, violet, and finally that deep, luminous blue that exists for only a few minutes before true darkness.

## Half-Year Review

Six months gone. Time to look back at my January intentions:

**Read more fiction:** ✓ I've read fourteen books this year, ten of them fiction. Each one has left a small mark on how I see the world.

**Write something every day:** Mostly. I missed maybe fifteen days total. But on the days I did write, I wrote with more depth and honesty than I ever have before.

**Spend more time in silence:** This has been the most transformative. Silence isn't empty. It's full of everything you've been too busy to hear.

## On Memory & Time

I've been thinking about how we experience time. In the moment, January feels impossibly far away. But when I read back through these journal entries, it all collapses — every month feels simultaneously distant and immediate.

Writing is a time machine. These words are breadcrumbs I'm leaving for my future self, small proofs that these moments existed and mattered.

## Summer Evening

Tonight: an open window, the sound of distant laughter, the smell of jasmine, a glass of wine, and this journal. Sometimes the simplest moments are the most complete.

> "Tell me, what is it you plan to do with your one wild and precious life?" — Mary Oliver

I don't have the full answer yet. But I'm getting closer. And the search itself might be the point.
